A · Classical planetary colours

  • Recognisable. A Jyotisha-literate user reads the strip without the labels — Saturn’s slate-grey and Jupiter’s gold are recognised at a glance.
  • Carries meaning. Colour itself encodes the planet’s nature (Mars red = active, Moon silver = receptive). Lagna ribbon’s elemental colouring (fire/earth/air/water) tells the user what kind of energy each lagna brings.
  • Differentiation. Seven distinct hues + four rashi elements give the eye real information density. Pattern-recognition is fast across a 24-cell strip.
  • Visual noise. Introduces seven new colour families to a page that today is calmly indigo/cream. Reads as a different app pinned next to the muhurta finder.
  • Brand drift. Site has built equity around the single saffron-maroon hue. Any future component is now asking “planetary, or site?”.
  • Subjective mappings. Mars red, Saturn black, Sun gold are standard. Mercury green / Venus white / Moon silver / Jupiter yellow vary by lineage — purists will push back on individual choices.
  • Accessibility cost. Seven hues at the saturation level shown need WCAG verification on text contrast; some pairings (Mercury green text on green bg) are borderline.

B · Site indigo palette

  • Coherent. Looks like it grew out of the existing UI. No visual seam between the new strips and the rest of the page.
  • Information stays in labels. Forces the planet glyph + name to do the identification work — which is arguably correct for users who don’t already read the classical colours.
  • Encodes favourability instead of identity. Saturation steps say “better / worse for you today”, which is the question users actually ask. Identity (which planet) is still in the glyph and text.
  • Cheap to extend. Personal lagna favourability, hora-vara match bonuses from the muhurta finder, “current hora” highlighting all slot into the same saturation language.
  • Lower information density. Three intensity steps is fewer than seven hues. Users can’t scan and read “that’s a Mars hour” without looking at the glyph.
  • Slower for experts. Devotees who know the classical scheme have to translate.
  • Benefic/malefic is a simplification. Sun is classed “neutral”, Saturn/Mars “malefic” — but a Saturn hora is the right hora for disciplined labour, not a “bad” hora. The site palette flattens that nuance to a single axis.

Recommendation

Ship Option B (site palette) as default and offer Option A as an opt-in toggle. Reason: most visitors are not yet Jyotisha-literate, and the site’s pedagogical value comes from showing what’s good for them (favourability) rather than what’s cosmically true (planetary identity). The classical scheme is a power-user view — wire it behind a “Classical view” toggle in the help drawer for the devotees who want it. That way you keep brand coherence without losing the experts.

Lagna ribbon: regardless of which palette wins for horas, lagna favours relative colouring (kendra/trikona from janma) over elemental colouring, because the elemental view is informationally weaker than the personal-fit view once you’ve already collected janma rasi in the Tarabalam panel.
